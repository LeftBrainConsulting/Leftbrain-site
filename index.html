Why AI stalls

The model was never
the hard part.

Building an agent is a weekend. Getting a company to run on one is a year — and almost everything that goes wrong in between is organisational.

95%of AI pilots show no measurable P&L impact
40%of agentic projects cancelled by end of 2027
7%of companies say their data is actually ready

Nobody picked

Twelve things started because twelve people asked. The three that mattered are competing for the same four people, and none of them will finish.

The process underneath was never written down

You can't automate a process that runs differently depending on who's doing it. Most AI projects discover this in month three.

Nobody owns it

It crosses IT, ops, security and a department. No single budget line, no single name, so it dies in committee rather than in production.

The build was sized, the readiness wasn't

Everyone estimates the model work. Nobody estimates the six weeks of defining and standardising that has to happen first.

It was built for people who never asked

A central team ships something to a department that didn't want it. Adoption becomes a change programme instead of a non-event.

So the second one is harder to fund than the first

A pilot that produced a nice demo and no number makes the next approval slower. That's how AI budgets quietly die.

Every one of those is a decision failure, and none of them is fixed by buying another tool.

The part nobody scores

An AI project isn't one job.
It's a jump between stages.

Every process sits somewhere on this track. What makes an AI initiative big or small isn't the model — it's how many stages it has to cross before an agent can run against it.

Stage 1

Undefined

Nobody has written down how this actually works today.

Stage 2

Documented

Written down, but still done differently by different people.

Stage 3

Standardised

One way of doing it. The exceptions are named rather than improvised.

Stage 4

Instrumented

It produces a record something else can actually read.

Stage 5

Automatable

An agent can run against it, and you'd trust the output.

Every initiative gets a current stage and a target stage. That single pair is the difference between a six-week project and an eighteen-month one — and it's the number that turns an ambition into a bandwidth conversation.

What gets installed

Ten working days.
You own all of it.

Your initiative portfolio doesn't exist as data anywhere — it lives in decks, heads and Slack threads. That's the real reason nothing can run on it.

One intake

A single door for every request that changes how the business operates — a tool, a process, an agent, a fix. Nothing becomes business as usual without coming through it.

One scoring standard

Impact, urgency and who's asking, on a fixed scale. Departments argue on one scale instead of five, and the guide defines what each level means.

Reasoning kept with the score

Every score carries the sentence that justifies it. It's why the ranking can be audited six months later, and why inflation gets caught.

Readiness staging

Current stage and target stage on every initiative, so effort reflects the work that actually has to happen rather than the build alone.

An honest capacity limit

What each team can absorb while still doing its day job, and who is already past it. One line, and the room goes quiet.

A decision log

What was decided, when, by whom, and why. The record your board asks for and almost nobody has — and the reason a no costs nothing to defend.

You keep the standard, the data and the cadence. It runs without the person who built it.
