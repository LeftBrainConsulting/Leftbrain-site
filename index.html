Who you’d be working with

Nobody hands you the title organizational architect.

I was put inside a Fortune 50 turnaround at 23 and handed the change control board — 50+ application development teams, 1,000+ executive change requests, on a $200M program overhauling a $2B business unit. It didn’t exist before me. The platform couldn’t go live without it.

Then I became the audit point of contact for that program, which meant seeing how every team actually operated against the standard rather than how they described it. Most operators know one function deeply. That year I got the cross-section — and that vantage point is the whole thing. I know how functions fail against each other, where decisions actually get made, and what has to exist before execution can hold.

Eight years, five promotions, Associate Director by 25. A $125M budget covering every enterprise system at a $15B company. A failed ERP consolidation I got handed the rollback of. A $100M ARR business unit running as three silos that I put behind one intake — still running today after the leadership that sponsored it turned over.

The short version

I build the layer that decides. Then I leave, and it keeps running. That constraint — that it has to survive without me — is what shaped every method on this page.
